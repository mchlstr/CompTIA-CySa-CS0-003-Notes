# Customer Breach Notification

> **Important:** legal must review and approve before sending. Specific wording, distribution timing, and required content vary by jurisdiction (GDPR, US state laws, HIPAA, sector regulations). This is a starting structure, not a substitute for legal review.

**Subject:** Important security notice regarding your `<service / account>`

---

Dear `<Customer name>`,

We are writing to inform you of a security incident that may have affected your information.

## What happened

On `<date discovered>`, we became aware that `<brief, factual description of the incident — what happened, not speculation about who or why>`. We took immediate action to `<contain / investigate / remediate>`.

## What information was involved

Based on our investigation, the following types of information about you may have been affected:

- `<category 1 — e.g., name, email>`
- `<category 2 — e.g., last 4 of card>`
- `<category 3 — only include what's confirmed>`

`<State explicitly what was NOT affected, if known and helpful — e.g., "Passwords were not compromised because they are stored in hashed form.">`

## What we are doing

- `<containment / eradication / recovery actions>`
- `<engagement of external incident response / forensic firm if applicable>`
- `<notification to law enforcement / regulators if applicable>`
- `<process and security improvements being made>`

## What you can do

- `<action 1 — e.g., reset your password>`
- `<action 2 — e.g., monitor your account for suspicious activity>`
- `<action 3 — e.g., enable MFA>`
- `<offer of credit monitoring / identity protection if applicable, with enrollment instructions>`

## For more information

If you have questions, please contact us:
- Email: `<dedicated incident inbox>`
- Phone: `<dedicated hotline, with hours>`
- FAQ / status page: `<URL>`

We sincerely regret any inconvenience this may cause. Protecting your information is a responsibility we take seriously, and we are committed to `<concrete commitment, e.g., strengthening our defenses, transparent communication>`.

Sincerely,

`<Name>`
`<Title>`
`<Organization>`

---

## References

The structure (what happened / what info was involved / what we are doing / what you can do / contact) is the **de facto standard for breach notification letters in the US and EU**. It is shaped by specific legal requirements:

- **GDPR Art. 33** — notification to supervisory authority within 72 hours: https://gdpr-info.eu/art-33-gdpr/
- **GDPR Art. 34** — notification to affected data subjects: https://gdpr-info.eu/art-34-gdpr/
- **HHS HIPAA Breach Notification Rule** (§§ 164.400–414) — specifies required content for individual notices: https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/breach-notification/index.html
- **California Civ. Code § 1798.82** — original US state breach notification law; sets the basic format used widely: https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=1798.82
- **California AG — Data Breach Reports** — public list of *real* breach notification letters submitted by companies; the best place to see actual industry examples: https://oag.ca.gov/privacy/databreach/list
- **SEC — Form 8-K Item 1.05** — public-company material cybersecurity incident disclosure: https://www.sec.gov/news/press-release/2023-139
- **NCSL — State Data Breach Notification Laws** — comparative summary of all US state requirements: https://www.ncsl.org/technology-and-communication/security-breach-notification-laws
- **NIST SP 800-122 — Guide to Protecting PII** (notification considerations): https://csrc.nist.gov/publications/detail/sp/800-122/final
